# Notes on four hook patterns

These notes come from reading more than 1000 viral hooks. Four patterns show up again and again.

Contradictions and contrast. A hook that holds two things that do not fit together creates tension, and your brain cannot scroll past unresolved tension. About 30% of the top performers used this pattern.

The specificity effect. The more weirdly specific a hook gets, the more people relate. Speak to one person instead of an audience. A generic hook says "If you ever get bloated after a meal". A specific hook says "If you've ever secretly unbuttoned your jeans at dinner and hoped no one noticed, this is for you".

Timeframe tension. Short, unexpected timeframes start a curiosity loop. "Three months ago I had 0 followers" gives the viewer hope that it is possible.

POVs as advice. The most engaging POV hooks are advice in disguise. People's defenses are down when they think they are just relating to a scenario, not receiving instruction.

Overall, the best hooks read like genuine human moments that someone just happened to articulate perfectly.
